# Paris Street; Rainy Day (1877)

**Artist:** Gustave Caillebotte  
**Location:** Art Institute of Chicago, Chicago  

### Overview and Imagery
*Paris Street; Rainy Day* (French: *Rue de Paris, temps de pluie*) is Gustave Caillebotte's best-known work. It shows a spacious intersection in north Paris near the Gare Saint-Lazare, populated by middle-class pedestrians holding umbrellas on a wet autumn afternoon.

### Visual Details
The painting is striking for its grand scale and deep, photographic perspective. The cobblestone streets are wet and slick, reflecting the gray, overcast sky. Pedestrians wearing dark, fashionable coats and top hats populate the scene, their black umbrellas adding geometric structures across the canvas. A couple walks toward the viewer in the foreground, seemingly cropped by the frame. Caillebotte’s lighting is diffuse, capturing the cool, soft atmosphere of a rainy day.

### Significance
Though Caillebotte used a more precise and realistic technique than Monet or Renoir, his work shares the Impressionist interest in light, modern cityscapes, and fleeting moments of everyday life. The cropping and wide-angle perspective mimic early street photography.
