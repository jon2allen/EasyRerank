# A Bar at the Folies-Bergère (1882)

**Artist:** Édouard Manet  
**Location:** Courtauld Gallery, London  

### Overview and Imagery
*A Bar at the Folies-Bergère* (French: *Un bar aux Folies-Bergère*) is the last major work completed by Édouard Manet before his death. The scene takes place at the famous Folies-Bergère music hall in Paris, focusing on a barmaid named Suzon standing behind a marble bar loaded with bottles of champagne, beer, and a bowl of oranges.

### Visual Details
The painting is famous for its complex and intriguing composition, which makes extensive use of a mirror behind the barmaid. The mirror reflects the vast, bustling crowd of the music hall, glowing chandeliers, and even the legs of a trapeze artist in the upper-left corner. Suzon's expression is detached, melancholy, and tired, contrasting sharply with the lively, glittering nightlife going on around her. The reflection of Suzon's back and her interaction with a male customer are shifted to the right, creating a spatial puzzle that has intrigued art historians for decades.

### Significance
Manet bridged the gap between Realism and Impressionism. This work exemplifies the themes of urban alienation, modern consumerism, and the spectacle of Parisian society.
