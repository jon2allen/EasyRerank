# Bal du moulin de la Galette (1876)

**Artist:** Pierre-Auguste Renoir  
**Location:** Musée d'Orsay, Paris  

### Overview and Imagery
*Bal du moulin de la Galette* is one of Impressionism's most celebrated masterpieces. It depicts a lively Sunday afternoon dance at the Moulin de la Galette in the Montmartre district of Paris, where working-class Parisians would dress up, socialize, dance, and drink galettes (a type of pancake).

### Visual Details
Renoir’s painting is a vibrant explosion of color, motion, and light. The composition features a crowd of figures talking and dancing under the trees. Renoir brilliantly captures the effect of dappled sunlight filtering through the foliage, casting flickering patches of light and shadow across the faces, clothing, and ground. The warm orange and pink hues of the outdoor lighting contrast with the deep blue shadows of the coats and dresses.

### Significance
The painting is a prime example of the Impressionist focus on modern life, leisure, and the sensory experiences of light and atmosphere. Renoir rejected traditional dark shadows and smooth finishes in favor of bright, feathered brushstrokes to convey the joyful energy of the scene.
