# Bridge at Villeneuve-la-Garenne (1872)

**Artist:** Alfred Sisley  
**Location:** Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York  

### Overview and Imagery
*Bridge at Villeneuve-la-Garenne* is one of Alfred Sisley's early landscape masterpieces. Painted in the summer of 1872, the work depicts the newly constructed cast-iron and stone suspension bridge over the Seine River in the suburbs of Paris.

### Visual Details
The painting is structured by the bold diagonal perspective of the suspension bridge extending from the right side of the canvas. Underneath the bridge, the quiet blue-green water reflects the sun-drenched sky, green trees, and houses. Holidaymakers in small boats enjoy the river, and pedestrians stroll across the bridge. Sisley’s brushwork captures the shimmering warmth of a summer afternoon, utilizing short, dry strokes for the stone piers and fluid, horizontal strokes for the water reflections.

### Significance
Sisley was the most dedicated pure landscape painter among the Impressionists. This work captures the combination of rural tranquility and modern industrial design, a favorite theme of early Impressionism.
