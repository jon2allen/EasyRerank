# The Child's Bath (1893)

**Artist:** Mary Cassatt  
**Location:** Art Institute of Chicago, Chicago  

### Overview and Imagery
*The Child's Bath* (French: *La toilette de l'enfant*) is a famous oil painting by American artist Mary Cassatt, who lived and worked in France as an active member of the Impressionist group. The painting portrays an intimate, domestic scene of a mother bathing a young child.

### Visual Details
The painting uses an unusual overhead angle, looking down at the figures. The mother gently supports the child whose feet are dipped into a small basin of water. Cassatt emphasizes flat planes, bold outlines, and decorative patterns—especially in the striped dress of the mother, the patterned rug, and the floral wallpaper. The colors are soft and natural, with white and pink flesh tones contrasting against the dark green and purple stripes of the dress.

### Significance
Cassatt’s work is deeply influenced by the flat perspective and decorative patterns of Japanese *ukiyo-e* woodblock prints. The painting celebrates the quiet, everyday moments of womanhood and childcare, moving away from formal portraits.
