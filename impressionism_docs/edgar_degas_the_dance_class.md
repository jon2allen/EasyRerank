# The Dance Class (1874)

**Artist:** Edgar Degas  
**Location:** Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York  

### Overview and Imagery
*The Dance Class* (French: *La classe de danse*) is one of Edgar Degas's most famous depictions of ballet dancers. Unlike other Impressionists who focused on outdoor landscapes and sunlight, Degas was fascinated by indoor movement, theater, and the human body in motion.

### Visual Details
The painting depicts a group of ballet dancers during a rehearsal overseen by the veteran dance master Jules Perrot. The dancers are scattered across the room in various states of exhaustion and preparation—some stretching, some adjusting their slippers, others slumping wearily. Degas utilizes a dramatic, tilted floor perspective that pulls the viewer's eye diagonally into the deep background. The cool green walls of the rehearsal room contrast with the delicate white and pink tutus of the young dancers.

### Significance
Degas was heavily influenced by photography and Japanese woodblock prints, which is evident in the off-center, asymmetrical framing of the dancers. His focus was on the hard, unglamorous work behind the beauty of the ballet performance, capturing candid and natural body poses.
