# Impression, Sunrise (1872)

**Artist:** Claude Monet  
**Location:** Musée Marmottan Monet, Paris  

### Overview and Imagery
*Impression, Sunrise* (French: *Impression, soleil levant*) is a historic oil painting by Claude Monet first shown at what would become known as the "Exhibition of the Impressionists" in Paris in April 1874. The painting is credited with inspiring the name of the Impressionist movement.

### Visual Details
The painting depicts the port of Le Havre, Monet's hometown, at sunrise. Monet painted the harbor using loose, rapid brushstrokes, capturing the fleeting light of early morning. Two small rowboats in the foreground guide the eye toward the center of the canvas. The brilliant orange sun stands out sharply against the cool, blue-gray mist of the harbor, reflecting a path of fiery light across the undulating water. 

### Significance
Rather than aiming for a realistic or detailed representation of the harbor, Monet focused on capturing the atmosphere, reflection of light, and color of a single transient moment. The loose style and name of the painting prompted art critic Louis Leroy to coin the term "Impressionism" in a satirical review.
