# The Cradle (1872)

**Artist:** Berthe Morisot  
**Location:** Musée d'Orsay, Paris  

### Overview and Imagery
*The Cradle* (French: *Le Berceau*) is Berthe Morisot's most famous painting. It shows the artist's sister, Edma, watching over her sleeping daughter, Blanche. It was exhibited at the first Impressionist exhibition in 1874, marking Morisot as a key figure in the movement.

### Visual Details
The painting depicts a quiet, intimate scene of maternal love. The composition is built around a series of diagonals and curves: Edma’s bent arm, the drape of the cradle's netting, and the diagonal line of the baby's arm mirroring her mother's pose. Morisot uses a delicate palette dominated by soft whites, light pinks, and deep grays. The translucent netting of the cradle is rendered with light, airy brushstrokes, creating a protective, dreamlike envelope around the sleeping child.

### Significance
As a female Impressionist, Morisot was barred from many public scenes, leading her to focus on private domestic life. This painting is admired for its psychological depth and subtle depiction of intimacy, restraint, and tenderness.
